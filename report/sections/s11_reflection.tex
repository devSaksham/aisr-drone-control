\section{Co-learning Reflection}
Each member answers three questions: what I learned on my own, what I learned from team-mates, and what I taught others.

\textbf{Saksham Agrawal (M1):} On my own I learned the Webots API and the coordinate frames, and why the control signs must be measured instead of assumed: the sign probe showed that a positive roll input gives a negative roll. From the control and trajectory members I learned how the attitude loop and the minimum-jerk reference use the state I estimate. I explained the sign probe and the Kalman filter to the others, including why differentiating noisy GPS fails.

\textbf{Khushi Gupta (M2):} On my own I learned PID tuning and anti-windup, and saw that the sample roll gain of 50 is unstable for step commands. From the others I learned about frames and trajectories. I taught the team how the attitude loop and the mixer work, and explained the roll-gain finding.

\textbf{Tanvi Singhal (M3):} On my own I learned trajectory polynomials and the body-frame rotation that turns a position error into roll and pitch. From the others I learned how the altitude and attitude loops and the sensors behave. I taught the team how the minimum-jerk reference works and why it starts from the measured velocity.

\textbf{Khyati Yadav (M4):} On my own I learned how to integrate the modules into two complete controllers and how the Webots supervisor applies wind and watches for crashes. From the others I learned the control theory behind each loop. I taught the team how to run the demo and the wind test.

\textbf{Ambrish Chauraiya (M5):} On my own I learned how to design fair experiments and metrics, including crash detection and path error. From the others I learned what each controller does and why. I taught the team how to read the result plots and tables.
