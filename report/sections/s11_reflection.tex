\section{Co-learning Reflection}
Each member answers three questions: what I learned on my own, what I learned from team-mates, and what I taught others.

\textbf{Saksham Agrawal (M1):} I learned the Webots API and its frames on my own, and wrote a sign probe that measures every control sign instead of assuming it. From Khushi and Tanvi I learned how the attitude loop and trajectories use my state estimate, so delay and noise in it matter. From Ambrish I learned what the analysis needs from my logs. I taught the team the sign probe and the Kalman filter. I also explained why our first Kalman setting crashed every noisy run until we retuned it.

\textbf{Khushi Gupta (M2):} On my own I learned PID design beyond P/PD: anti-windup, a filtered derivative, and tilt compensation in the altitude loop. From Saksham I learned that frames must be measured, since our old attitude sign was inverted. From Tanvi I learned how smooth references change what the attitude loop must track. I taught the team the attitude loop and mixer. I also explained the roll-gain finding. With step commands the sample's roll gain crashes at 40 and 50, but with smooth trajectories every gain from 15 to 50 completes.

\textbf{Tanvi Singhal (M3):} On my own I learned minimum-jerk trajectories and how to turn world-frame position errors into body-frame commands. From Saksham I learned the frame conventions. From Khushi I learned the tilt and thrust limits of the attitude loop, so I tuned speeds to stay inside them. I taught the team the minimum-jerk reference. It removes the sudden jumps of step waypoints and lowers the demand on the attitude loop.

\textbf{Khyati Yadav (M4):} On my own I learned how to integrate the modules into one controller, and how the Webots supervisor applies crosswind. From Saksham, Khushi and Tanvi I learned each module's interface, which was where most integration bugs came from. From Ambrish I learned what the experiments need from the main loop. I taught the team how to run the demo with custom waypoints. I also explained the wind test, where both controllers fly the same scenario so the comparison is fair.

\textbf{Ambrish Chauraiya (M5):} On my own I learned how to design fair experiments: identical scenarios for both methods, ten noise seeds, and crashes counted as failures. From Saksham I learned what is logged, from Khushi and Tanvi what each module changes, and from Khyati how to run each scenario. I taught the team how to read the result plots, meaning error, overshoot, control effort and success rate. I also showed how to tell a real improvement from noise.
